## Report: Interest in AI Coding Assistants in Spanish & Portuguese Markets

### Answer
Interest in GitHub Copilot is **declining** in both Spanish and Portuguese Wikipedia editions. Spanish shows a relative share loss of −56% year-over-year; Portuguese shows −63%.

### Data Summary
| Language | Verdict | Relative YoY | Raw YoY | Est. Human Views/month | Trust |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Portuguese | Declining | −63% | −71% | ~27 | Medium (45) |

### Key Findings
- **Both editions losing share**: The topic's share of Wikipedia views in each language edition fell significantly year-over-year, showing declining curiosity about Copilot specifically
- **Very high bot contamination**: Spanish article is 48% automated traffic; Portuguese is 68%. This inflates uncertainty in the human-only trend
- **Very low human volume**: Only ~131 views/month in Spanish, ~27 in Portuguese (after bot filtering). Small numbers swing wildly, reducing trust
- **Consistent downward trend**: R² = 0.68–0.72 indicates the decline is steady, not a random spike

### What Wikipedia Data Shows
Wikipedia measures curiosity about the *product by name* (GitHub Copilot), not demand for AI-powered coding features in general. A Spanish or Portuguese speaker looking for coding help might search Stack Overflow, GitHub docs, or YouTube rather than Wikipedia.

### Recommendation for Your Team
Before building Spanish/Portuguese support, validate demand separately via:
1. **Google Trends / keyword research** for "AI code assistant," "copiloto de código," "assistente de código" to confirm market demand
2. **Landing page test** in Spanish/Portuguese measuring signup interest
3. **Developer surveys** in those markets

Wikipedia interest declining doesn't necessarily mean market demand is low — it reflects where people research this *specific product*, not whether they want AI coding features. Recommend treating this as a signal to validate with your own market research, not as a go/no-go decision.
